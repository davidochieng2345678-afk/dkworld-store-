Blazer Alteration Questions Answered — Expert Advice from 14 Years of Tailoring

Real answers from owner David Ochieng, who has spent over a decade altering blazers at Manix Clothing Store, Italian Men's Wear, and City Walk Ltd.

Can a blazer be altered to fit perfectly?

Yes — absolutely. Almost any blazer can be altered to fit perfectly, within limits. Sleeves, waist, length, buttons, and lining can all be adjusted. Shoulders are the most complex alteration because they determine the entire drape of the jacket, but with the right tailor (and the right technique), shoulders can be reduced or reshaped by 1–2 inches without ruining the jacket's structure. The only truly non-alterable elements are the overall fabric quantity (if the blazer is too small, we may not be able to let it out) and severe fabric damage.

How to know if your blazer needs altering?

Here are the signs your blazer needs altering: (1) The sleeve ends past your wrist bone — it should end at the wrist bone, showing 1 cm of shirt cuff. (2) The waist is too loose and billows at the sides. (3) The shoulders extend past your natural shoulder bone. (4) The blazer length doesn't cover your seat properly. (5) Buttons feel loose or are missing. (6) The lining is torn or shows through the fabric. (7) You feel constricted when you button the jacket. If any of these describe your blazer, a professional alteration will transform how you look.

Can you alter a blazer's shoulders?

Yes — but it's the most complex blazer alteration and requires an experienced tailor. Shoulder alteration involves opening the jacket at the shoulder seam, reducing or reshaping the padding, adjusting the armhole, and re-stitching everything so the jacket maintains its structure. A badly done shoulder alteration is very obvious — the jacket will twist, pucker, or lose its shape. David Ochieng has performed hundreds of shoulder alterations over fourteen years. Blazer shoulder alteration cost in Kenya starts from KSh 2,500.

What is the difference between blazer alteration and tailoring?

Blazer alteration modifies an existing blazer — shortening sleeves, taking in the waist, adjusting shoulders, replacing buttons. Bespoke tailoring creates a new blazer from scratch, cut to your exact measurements. Alteration is faster (1–3 days) and more affordable; bespoke is a 3–4 week process and costs significantly more. For most men, alteration of an existing quality blazer is the best value — you keep the fabric and structure you already own while getting a perfect fit.
